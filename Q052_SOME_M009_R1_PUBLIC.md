# A missing interval, recovered

## Research status

RESEARCH IN PROGRESS — INCONCLUSIVE.

We are comparing two cosmology calculations that differ in one tiny contribution from ionized helium. The aim is to establish both what their saved electron-fraction and light-scattering curves show and how accurately those curves represent the underlying equations.

The stored optical-depth curve was already checked continuously over its exported range. But the saved table stopped just before redshift810, leaving the final short interpolation interval undocumented. A new controlled pair now exports the missing upper spline data and the conformal-time coordinate at810.

Every previously exported table row and cumulative history value matched the earlier calculation exactly. The newly captured upper coefficients belong to the new run. Matching the overlap does not prove what the earlier run’s unexported coefficients were.

This supplies the data needed for the next mathematical check. It does not complete the global error certificate or establish the exact maximum difference between the underlying model-equation solutions. The mathematical bounds still depend on stated background and starting-state assumptions.

## Short video script

We’ve been checking something easy to overlook in a cosmology calculation: how much of a plotted curve is actually backed by saved data and a mathematical error bound.

Two runs differ in a tiny helium contribution. We had checked the saved light-scattering curve over its exported range. But the table stopped just before our comparison boundary. The spline needed one more point above that boundary, and we didn’t have it.

We’ve now recovered the original numerical library and run one controlled pair with an extra data export. Every previously saved row matched the earlier calculation. The new export supplies the missing upper cell and its coordinate mapping.

That is progress in documenting the calculation. The next step is to finish the mathematical check through the boundary. The full accuracy question is still unresolved.
